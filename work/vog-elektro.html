01 Overview

The brief

VOG Elektro is an electrical installation company in Belgrade offering installations, repairs, lighting and smart home solutions. They needed a professional website that explains their services clearly, shows their work, and makes it easy for customers to call them.

My role

I handled the whole project on my own: the visual design, the information structure, all of the website copy, the front-end code, SEO, and setting up the domain and hosting. The client provided the project photographs.

I designed the site directly in the browser, iterating on layout, spacing and typography in code rather than in a separate design tool.

02 Design decisions

Built around one action: the call

  1. Calling is always one tap away

    Most customers of an electrician want to talk to someone right away. The phone number and a “Call us” button sit in the sticky header, in the hero and again in a closing call-to-action, so the primary action is never more than one tap away.

  2. A single, scannable page

    The content lives on one page with clear sections — Services, Why us, About, Projects and Contact — linked from the navigation. Visitors can scroll through the whole story or jump straight to what they need.

  3. Trust before the sale

    Short, concrete points — fast arrival, reliable work, transparent pricing and a guarantee — plus counters for experience and completed projects help a first-time visitor decide to call.

  4. A swipeable project gallery

    Past work is shown in a horizontal, swipeable carousel. It keeps the page compact on mobile, and each project links to the company's Instagram, where new work is published.

  5. SEO for local search

    Page title and headings are written around how people actually search locally (for example, “Električar Beograd”), on top of a semantic HTML structure and descriptive page metadata.

  6. Copy written for the customer

    I wrote all text in Serbian for the local audience, with short headings and plain language that explains each service without technical jargon.
